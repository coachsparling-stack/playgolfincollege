One update

College golf's fall signing period opens Wednesday, November 11. That's the first day a high school senior can sign a written athletics aid agreement with a Division I or Division II school. The National Letter of Intent is gone, so each school now writes its own agreement. Before your senior signs, read it together: how much aid, for how long, and what conditions apply. And if your child isn't signing this fall, that's normal. Many players find their school in the spring or summer of senior year.

One more date to know: the Division I golf transfer window runs December 1 to 15. College players who move can change how many roster spots a coach has for next fall, so it's fair to ask coaches about it.

One thought: the scholarship isn't the prize

Every November, families start measuring a whole junior career by one day. Signing day photos fill up social media, and if your child isn't in one, it can feel like you're behind. You're not.

I've seen this from both sides, as a college coach at Duke and Ohio State and as the parent of three Division I golfers. Signing day is a snapshot. Development is the whole story.

In Becoming Elite, Leo's journey ends with this line: "He realized that the scholarship was not the prize. The prize was the person he had become in the process of earning it."

That's true whether your child signs this fall, next spring, or never plays college golf at all. The habits they build, how they handle a bad round, how they treat people, and whether they still love the game are what last. Those are also exactly what college coaches are watching for.

So if signing season stirs up some anxiety in your house this month, keep the long view. Your job isn't to hurry the timeline. It's to keep the light steady while they grow into the player, and the person, they're becoming.

Three lines worth keeping

"Mistakes aren't verdicts. They're just data."

"Parents: your role is support, not stress."

"Players watch what you do far more than they listen to what you say."

One question for your family

What's one thing you've gotten better at this year that doesn't show up on a scorecard?

Ask it at dinner or in the car, and let your child answer first.

Be a lighthouse, not a tugboat.
