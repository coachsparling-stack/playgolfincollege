title: Can Your Junior Golfer Accept Prize Money and Still Play College Golf? date: 2026-07-15 issue: 3 description: NCAA eligibility and golf amateur status are not the same thing. What changed in April, what didn't, and three questions to answer before accepting a check. ---

A parent asks: "My child has an opportunity to play in a professional event. Can they accept prize money and still play college golf?"

The answer has changed. But there are two separate sets of rules parents need to understand. NCAA eligibility and golf amateur status are not the same thing.

On April 15, 2026, the NCAA announced a Division I rule change allowing prospects to accept prize money in their sport before college enrollment without affecting eligibility. The announcement applies to prospects enrolling during the 2026 to 2027 academic year. It does not change the rules governing prize money received after college enrollment. [1]

That is a significant change. It is not, however, unrestricted permission to collect a professional tournament check.

Golf has its own Rules of Amateur Status. Under those rules, an amateur may generally accept up to $1,000 in total prizes per scratch competition, including cash. Cash prizes are not allowed in handicap competitions. [2]

There is another distinction: playing against professionals is different from entering and playing as a professional. The latter causes a player to lose amateur status. An event may also prohibit amateur entrants from accepting prize money altogether. [3]

Before accepting a payment, get answers to three questions

  • Does it comply with the NCAA rules for the division and enrollment circumstances involved?
  • Will accepting it affect the player's golf amateur status?
  • Do the tournament and the player's high school association permit it?

Send the actual event terms and proposed payment to the college's compliance office. Confirm amateur status questions with the USGA and high school eligibility questions with the appropriate state association.

Keep the answers and payment records.

The opportunity to earn money may be real. So are the consequences of confusing one organization's permission with everyone else's.

Be a lighthouse, not a tugboat.
