title: Two Dates That Just Changed Everything (and the One Thing That Didn't) date: 2026-06-16 issue: 1 description: June 15 opens Division I contact and August 1 opens visits. What each date means for a class of 2028 family, and how to set up a visit the right way. ---

If you have a junior golfer in the class of 2028, this week mattered more than you might realize. June 15 came and went, and the recruiting world quietly shifted. Let me walk you through what actually happened, what's coming on August 1, and how to handle both without losing your mind or your kid's trust.

June 15: the phone can finally ring

For Division I, June 15 after sophomore year is the first day college coaches can contact your child directly. Calls, texts, emails, DMs, even verbal offers. Before this date they legally couldn't reach out, no matter how interested they were in your kid.

Here's what trips parents up: if June 15 came and your phone stayed silent, that does not mean your kid isn't on anyone's list. Coaches have been researching and ranking players for over a year. Some reach out day one. Many wait until after they watch summer results. Silence in week one is not a verdict. It's the opening minute of a long process.

(Worth knowing: D2 coaches can communicate any time, D3 and NAIA set their own timelines. The June 15 rule is a D1 rule.)

August 1: the door to campus opens

August 1 before junior year is the next gate. That's when your child can begin taking official and unofficial visits.

The quick version: an unofficial visit is one you pay for and can take any time. An official visit is paid for by the school, and your kid gets one per program, with the visit window opening August 1. This is the date that turns "interested" into "come see us."

How to actually set up a visit

When a coach shows interest, the instinct is for you to jump in and run logistics. Resist it. Here's the order that works:

Let your kid send the email asking about a visit. Coaches notice who's driving the bus, and they want it to be the player, not the parent. Build the visit around a tournament when you can, so the coach sees competitive golf, not just a campus tour. And come with questions ready: what does a normal week look like, how does he handle a player in a slump, what happens academically if golf goes sideways.

Your job on the visit is to observe, not interview. Watch how the coach talks to current players. Watch how your kid lights up or doesn't. You'll learn more from the body language in the room than from any pitch.

The one thing that hasn't changed

The rules get the headlines, but the game underneath them is the same one it's always been. Your kid still has to want this. They still have to own the emails, the practice, the hard conversations. Your role through all of it is steadier and quieter than the recruiting noise makes it feel.

When the texts start coming and the visits get scheduled, the temptation to grab the wheel will be enormous. Don't. Light the way and let them sail toward it.

Be a lighthouse, not a tugboat.
